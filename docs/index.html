Abstract

We study a learned continuous-time dynamical system whose symmetric-Jacobian eigenvalues are held inside a two-sided band by construction. The upper end of the band makes the system contracting, so trajectories converge to one another and the system is stable; the lower end keeps the backward expansion at rate λmax, making exact time inversion numerically safe. A free skew-symmetric term adds rotation — the curved, non-monotone component real trajectories require — without disturbing the contraction rate.

Fixing the whole eigenvalue spectrum inside an explicit, editable band endows the system with a set of properties we establish as theorems: stability and convergence to a unique equilibrium; exact time inversion: integrating the negated field runs the system backward at rate λmax; and post-hoc spectral reshaping that retimes behaviour with a bounded, predictable deviation.

On the LASA handwriting benchmark the system fits demonstrated skills, contracts to the goal from arbitrary starts, and replays backward with near-solver-precision error, at accuracy comparable to contractive-imitation baselines that offer neither retiming nor exact inversion.

Index Terms: contracting dynamics, bi-Lipschitz, imitation learning, time inversion, contraction theory, stable dynamical systems

Key Properties

  • Stable & convergent — every trajectory converges to a unique equilibrium at rate λmin.
  • Exactly invertible — integrating the negated field retraces the forward path at rate λmax, with near-solver-precision error.
  • Re-shapeable post-hoc — clipping the eigenvalue band retimes a trained skill without retraining, with a bounded trajectory deviation.
  • Pairwise contraction — BiLip-MLP generators certify λmin − sR and a positive injectivity margin for the full Jacobian.
